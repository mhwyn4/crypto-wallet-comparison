## What should you compare before choosing a crypto wallet?

The word “wallet” hides several different products. Comparing them only by the number of supported coins is a quick way to choose badly.

### 1. Custody model

A custodial wallet is controlled by a platform. The platform manages the private keys, while you access the account through login credentials and security checks.

A self-custody wallet gives you control of the private keys or seed phrase. That means the wallet provider generally cannot restore access if you lose the recovery information. OKX describes its Web3 Wallet as non-custodial, with users managing their own keys and recovery information.

Self-custody removes one type of counterparty risk, but it creates another responsibility: your seed phrase becomes critical. Anyone who obtains it can potentially control the assets associated with it. If you lose it, recovery may be impossible.

### 2. Hot wallet versus cold wallet

A hot wallet is connected to the internet through an app, browser extension, or Web3 interface. It is convenient for transactions, but the device and wallet environment are exposed to online threats.

A cold wallet, usually a hardware wallet, keeps transaction signing separate from the internet-connected device. It is generally more appropriate for assets that you do not need to move often.

This does not mean a hardware wallet makes every transaction safe. Users can still approve a malicious contract or send funds to an incorrect address. Hardware storage protects the signing environment; it does not replace transaction verification.

### 3. Network support

A wallet may support a token only on certain networks. USDT on Ethereum is not the same transfer route as USDT on Tron, Solana, or another network. The destination wallet and the sending wallet must support the same network for the transfer to work as intended.

OKX states that its Wallet supports more than 80 networks and can recognize supported networks when connecting to decentralized applications. Its broader developer documentation also lists support across EVM networks, Bitcoin, Solana, TON, Sui, Aptos, Cosmos, Tron, and other ecosystems, although support can differ between wallet products and DApp connection methods.

Treat “multi-chain” as a starting point, not a guarantee. Before transferring funds, check:

1. The exact asset.
2. The exact network.
3. Whether the receiving wallet supports that network.
4. Whether you have enough native gas token to complete the transaction.

### 4. DApp and trading support

Some wallets are mainly designed to store and send assets. Others include token swaps, DApp discovery, staking interfaces, NFT tools, portfolio tracking, and cross-chain functions.

OKX Wallet presents itself as a broader Web3 interface with storage, swaps, on-chain trading, earning features, market information, and WalletConnect compatibility. The official product page also highlights access to decentralized liquidity pools and DApps.

That convenience is useful for users who would otherwise install several wallet extensions and switch between multiple websites. It also means you should pay attention to the transaction approval screen instead of clicking through automatically. A wallet can display a legitimate DApp and still allow you to sign a transaction you did not fully understand.

## How OKX Wallet compares with other wallet types

### OKX Wallet versus a hardware wallet

A hardware wallet is generally the stronger choice for long-term storage of larger balances because transaction signing occurs through a dedicated device.

OKX Wallet is more convenient for:

- Frequent swaps.
- DApp interaction.
- NFT activity.
- Mobile use.
- Multi-chain portfolio management.
- Moving assets between exchange and Web3 services.

A hardware wallet is more convenient for:

- Infrequent transactions.
- Long-term holding.
- Separating important funds from daily Web3 activity.
- Adding a physical approval step before signing.

Many users do not need to choose only one. A reasonable structure is to keep a limited operating balance in a hot wallet and store longer-term funds separately. The amount should reflect your own risk tolerance and the consequences of loss.

### OKX Wallet versus an exchange account

An exchange account is custodial. You log in, and the platform manages the wallet infrastructure behind the account.

OKX Wallet is self-custodial. You manage the wallet’s recovery material and sign on-chain transactions yourself. The two products can work together, but they solve different problems:

- Exchange account: buying, selling, order books, and account-based trading.
- Web3 wallet: on-chain ownership, DApps, swaps, NFTs, and blockchain transactions.

Keeping funds on an exchange may be simpler for active trading, but it exposes you to platform, account-access, withdrawal, and jurisdiction-related risks. Moving everything to self-custody removes the exchange custody layer but makes backup and transaction security your responsibility.

### OKX Wallet versus a single-chain wallet

A single-chain wallet can be easier to understand. If you use only Bitcoin, a Bitcoin-focused wallet may have fewer settings and fewer opportunities to send funds through the wrong network.

A multi-chain wallet becomes more useful when your activity includes several ecosystems. The tradeoff is complexity: different networks use different address formats, gas tokens, confirmation times, and transaction types.

Choose breadth only when you will actually use it. “Supports hundreds of networks” is not automatically better if you only hold one asset and rarely transact.

## Common mistakes that make a good wallet unsafe

The most common failures are operational rather than technical.

### Keeping the seed phrase online

Convenience creates a single point of failure. If a cloud account, email account, or phone backup is compromised, the seed phrase may be exposed.

### Confusing a wallet address with a network

An address that looks correct is not enough. Always verify the network and asset type before sending.

### Signing without reading

A user may install a reputable wallet and still lose funds by approving a malicious contract. The wallet name does not make every connected DApp trustworthy.

### Treating a fee rebate as a profit

A trading-fee discount reduces one trading cost. It does not protect against volatility, leverage losses, liquidation, spread, funding, or bad execution.

### Using one wallet for everything

Combining savings, daily transactions, NFT activity, unknown DApps, and experimental protocols in one wallet increases the impact of a single mistake. Separation is often easier to manage than trying to make one wallet do every job.
